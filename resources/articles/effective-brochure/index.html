Developing an Effective Brochure

A well-crafted brochure remains one of the most persuasive marketing assets in B2B sales. Here is how to structure, write, and spec yours for press.

Design & Strategy 6 Min Read 300 DPI · 0.125" Bleed
Home / Resources / Articles / Developing an Effective Brochure

Start with a Clear Objective

Before you lay out a single panel, define the one action you want the brochure to drive. Is it meant to prompt a sales inquiry, qualify prospects at a trade show, or leave behind a high-trust capabilities overview? Your objective dictates fold format, copy hierarchy, paper weight, and distribution.

Know Your Audience

An effective brochure speaks directly to its target buyer. Identify who will hold this piece and what they need to understand in the first ten seconds. A brochure built for a procurement officer reads and looks very differently from one designed for a hospitality guest or retail customer.

Choose the Right Fold Format

Each commercial fold style organizes information differently:

Tri-Fold (Letter) 6 panels; fits standard racks and #10 envelopes.
Bi-Fold (Half-Fold) 4 larger panels ideal for hero photography and tables.
Z-Fold / Accordion Opens flat for timelines, maps, and step-by-step guides.
Gatefold Dual outer flaps open to a high-impact center spread.

Write Compelling, Scannable Copy

Brochure real estate is compact, so every paragraph has to earn its space:

  • Lead with outcomes, not features. Frame your headline around what the buyer gains, then back it up with specs.
  • Write for skimmers. Most readers scan subheadings and bullet lists before committing to body copy.
  • Keep paragraphs tight. Two to four sentences per block leaves room for generous white space and clean typography.
  • Anchor a single CTA. Give the reader one primary next step: a phone number, quote URL, or trackable QR code.
  • Proofread on hard copy. Have two reviewers check phone numbers, URLs, and pricing before files hit press.

Design & Paper Selection

  • Use crisp 300 DPI CMYK photography and vector logos so every detail prints razor-sharp.
  • Match paper weight and finish to brand positioning, such as 80 lb or 100 lb gloss/silk text for mailers, or 100 lb cover with scoring for hand-distributed pieces.
  • Protect dark ink coverage across folds with aqueous coating or soft-touch lamination and mechanical scoring.
