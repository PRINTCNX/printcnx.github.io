Understanding Paper Weights

Paper is categorized by its weight, coating, and finish. In the United States, paper weight is expressed in pounds (lb.) and refers to the weight of 500 sheets measured at the paper's basis size — which varies by paper category. This is why a "60 lb. Cover" stock is dramatically heavier than "60 lb. Text" — they are weighed on different basis sheet sizes.

The metric equivalent is GSM (grams per square meter), which provides a universal comparison across all paper types since it uses a consistent measurement unit. Use the conversion chart below to translate between the two systems.

💡
Text vs. Cover — What's the Difference?

Text stocks (also called "book" or "offset" paper) are lighter and more flexible — used for the interior pages of booklets, brochures, and letterhead. Cover stocks are heavier and stiffer — used for business cards, postcard backs, folder covers, and any application requiring rigidity. A 60 lb. Cover stock is approximately equivalent to 163 gsm, while a 60 lb. Text is only about 90 gsm. When in doubt, ask for a paper sample — we keep a sample kit on hand for all common stocks.

Paper Coatings

The coating on a paper determines its surface texture, print quality, and sheen. Coatings are applied to the paper at the mill and affect how ink absorbs and dries on the surface.

✨

Gloss Coated

High-sheen coating that makes colors pop and images vibrant. Reduces readability at very small text sizes. Best for image-heavy marketing materials, photo books, and magazine-style pieces.

📃

Matte Coated

Smooth, low-sheen finish that is easy to read and write on. Colors are rich but more subdued. Preferred for text-heavy pieces like reports, booklets, and direct mail.

🎞️

Satin / Silk

The middle ground between gloss and matte. Provides a soft sheen with good color vibrancy and readability. Increasingly popular for high-end brochures and corporate materials.

📄

Uncoated

Natural, porous paper surface ideal for stationery, envelopes, and writing tablets. Absorbs ink more than coated stocks — classic, tactile feel. Affects color density.

Post-Press Finishes

Applied after printing, finishes add protection, premium feel, and visual impact. These are press-side or bindery-applied treatments distinct from the paper's base coating.

💧

Aqueous Coating

Water-based coating applied inline on press. Provides protection and a smooth finish. Available in gloss, satin, and matte. Fast-drying and eco-friendly.

🔆

UV Coating

High-gloss varnish cured by ultraviolet light. Extremely shiny and durable — resists scuffing and fingerprints. Can be applied as flood coat or as spot UV for dramatic contrast.

🫱

Soft-Touch Laminate

Velvety, suede-like matte laminate that is incredibly tactile and premium. Highly popular for luxury business cards, packaging, and cosmetic materials.

🪞

Gloss Laminate

Clear, high-gloss film bonded to the printed surface. Provides maximum color vibrancy and excellent durability. Common on booklet covers and presentation folders.

🖐️

Matte Laminate

Flat, non-reflective film that is easy to write on and resists fingerprints. Popular for booklet covers, menus, and presentation materials.

⭐

Foil Stamping

Metallic or pigmented foil transferred to paper using heat and pressure. Available in gold, silver, copper, and holographic. Adds luxury shimmer to business cards and book covers.

Paper Weight Conversion Chart

Each row represents the same physical paper thickness expressed in different U.S. weight categories and the metric GSM equivalent. Use this table to compare weights across types or to match an international spec to a U.S. equivalent.

U.S. Weight Categories
  • Bond / Ledger — basis size 17×22″. Used for writing, copying, stationery.
  • Offset / Text — basis size 25×38″. Used for commercial printing, books, brochures.
  • Cover — basis size 20×26″. Used for business cards, covers, postcards.
  • Tag — basis size 24×36″. Used for tags, tickets, index cards.
  • Index — basis size 25.5×30.5″. Used for filing, cards, tabbed dividers.
Metric Columns
  • Points — thickness in thousandths of an inch (pts = caliper × 1000).
  • Caliper (in) — actual sheet thickness in inches.
  • Millimeters — thickness in millimeters.
  • GSM — grams per square meter. The universal metric weight standard.
