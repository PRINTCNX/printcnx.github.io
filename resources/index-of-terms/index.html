Index of Printing Terms

A comprehensive glossary of printing and graphic design terminology from A to Z.

43 terms
A B C D E F G H I L O P R S T U V W
A
Aqueous Coating
A water-based coating applied to printed materials for protection and sheen. Available in gloss, satin, and matte finishes. Dries quickly and is more environmentally friendly than solvent-based coatings.
B
Bleed
Artwork that extends beyond the trim edge of a document to ensure color or images reach the edge after cutting. Standard bleed is 1/8" (0.125") on all sides.
C
CMYK
Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Black: the four ink colors used in standard four-color process (offset and digital) printing. All color print files should be converted to CMYK mode before submission.
Coated Stock
Paper that has been treated with a coating (clay or polymer) on one or both sides to create a smoother printing surface. Available in gloss, satin, silk, and matte finishes.
Color Separation
The process of separating a full-color image into individual CMYK components, each of which is printed using a separate plate on an offset press.
Crop Marks
Lines printed outside the image area that indicate where the printed sheet should be trimmed. Also called "trim marks."
D
Die Cut
A custom shape cut into printed material using a metal die. Used to create unique shapes for business cards, packaging, labels, and promotional items.
DPI (Dots Per Inch)
A measure of printing resolution. Commercial printing typically requires 300 DPI or higher for sharp, high-quality output. Web images are typically 72 DPI and unsuitable for print.
Duotone
A halftone image reproduced using two ink colors, typically black plus one Pantone color, to add depth and tonal range to grayscale images.
E
Embossing
A finishing technique that creates a raised impression in paper by pressing it between two dies. Debossing creates a recessed impression. Both add a premium tactile dimension to print pieces.
EPS (Encapsulated PostScript)
A vector file format that can contain both vector and raster graphics. Widely used in the print industry for logos and illustrations that need to scale without loss of quality.
F
Foil Stamping
A finishing process that applies metallic or holographic foil to a printed surface using heat and pressure. Common on business cards, book covers, and luxury packaging.
Four-Color Process
Standard printing method using CMYK inks to reproduce the full spectrum of colors. Also called "process color" or "full-color" printing.
G
Gang Printing
A production technique where multiple different jobs are printed on the same press sheet simultaneously to reduce cost. Common in digital and short-run printing.
Gripper Edge
The leading edge of a press sheet that is held by the press grippers. No printing should appear in this area, typically 1/2" from the edge.
GSM (Grams per Square Meter)
The metric measurement of paper weight. Higher GSM indicates a heavier, thicker paper. Common in international specifications.
H
Halftone
A printing technique that simulates continuous-tone images (like photographs) by breaking them into a pattern of dots of varying sizes. Larger dots represent darker tones.
I
Imposition
The arrangement of pages on a press sheet so that when the sheet is printed, folded, and trimmed, the pages appear in the correct sequence.
Inkjet Printing
A digital printing method that sprays tiny droplets of ink onto paper. Used in wide-format printing and some short-run digital applications.
L
Laminate
A thin protective film bonded to the surface of printed materials. Available in gloss, matte, and soft-touch finishes. Adds durability and visual appeal.
Lamination
A thin film applied over printed material to protect the surface and enhance durability or appearance, available in gloss, matte, and soft-touch finishes.
LPI (Lines Per Inch)
The measure of halftone screen frequency. Higher LPI produces finer, more detailed halftones. Commercial offset printing typically uses 150–200 LPI.
O
Offset Printing
A lithographic printing process where ink is transferred from a plate to a rubber blanket, then to the paper. The standard for high-volume, high-quality commercial printing.
Overprint
Printing one ink color on top of another rather than knocking out (removing) the underlying color. Used intentionally for trapping or special effects.
P
Pantone (PMS)
Pantone Matching System: a standardized color system used to specify and reproduce precise ink colors across different printers and materials. Essential for brand color consistency.
PDF/X
A subset of the PDF standard specifically designed for print production. PDF/X-1a and PDF/X-4 are the most common formats for press-ready file submission.
Perfect Binding
A bookbinding method where pages are glued together at the spine. Used for paperback books, catalogs, and magazines.
Prepress
All the preparation steps that occur before a job goes on press, including design, typesetting, color separation, imposition, and plate-making.
R
Raster Image
A digital image composed of a grid of pixels. Raster images (JPEG, PNG, PSD, TIFF) cannot be scaled up without loss of quality. Minimum 300 DPI required for print.
Registration
The precise alignment of two or more printed colors on a sheet. Poor registration results in blurry edges and color fringing.
RGB
Red, Green, Blue: the additive color model used for screens and monitors. RGB files must be converted to CMYK before printing to avoid unexpected color shifts.
S
Saddle Stitch
A bookbinding method where folded sheets are stapled through the spine. Common for booklets, catalogs, and magazines under ~80 pages.
Score
A crease or groove pressed into paper or card stock to facilitate folding without cracking. Essential for heavier stocks.
Soft Proof
A digital on-screen preview of how a printed piece will look. Useful for reviewing layout and color, but should not replace a hard-copy press proof for critical color approval.
Spot Color
A specific premixed ink (usually a Pantone color) printed in a single impression, as opposed to being built from CMYK process colors.
Stock
The paper or substrate on which a job is printed. Stock selection significantly impacts color, feel, and overall quality of the final printed piece.
T
Trapping
A prepress technique that creates slight overlaps between adjacent colors to compensate for minor misregistration on press.
Trim Size
The final dimensions of a printed piece after it has been cut. The trim size is smaller than the bleed size.
U
UV Coating
A high-gloss protective coating cured by ultraviolet light. Provides a very shiny, durable finish and excellent scuff resistance.
V
Variable Data Printing (VDP)
A form of digital printing that allows elements (text, images, graphics) to change from one piece to the next without stopping the press. Used for personalized direct mail.
Vector Graphics
Artwork created from mathematical paths rather than pixels. Vector files (AI, EPS, SVG) can be scaled to any size without loss of quality, which is essential for logos.
Vignette
A gradual fade from a tone or color to white or another color. Used as a design element or photographic effect.
W
Web Press
A high-speed printing press that prints on a continuous roll (web) of paper rather than individual sheets. Used for very high-volume jobs like newspapers and magazines.
